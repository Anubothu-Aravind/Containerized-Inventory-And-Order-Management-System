# Design Guidelines & Context

## Design Context

### Users
- **Operations & Warehouse Staff (Admin/Staff)**: Need high information density, clear operational flow states, easy customer lookup/assignment, and fast order updates. They run operations on desktop screens.
- **Retail Customers (Customer)**: Need a clean, inviting, distraction-free product catalog, simple adding to cart, transparent simulated payment details, and instant order tracking. They might access from desktop or mobile devices.

### Brand Personality
- **Warm & Organic**: Moving away from sterile corporate blue/cyan, using warm terracotta, rich cream tones, and dark charcoal neutrals.
- **Utilitarian & Tactile**: Interface elements feel solid, physical, with subtle borders and clear interactive states (hover and active shifts).
- **Trustworthy & Transparent**: Transparent order statuses, detailed timelines, clear audit trails, and clear simulated sandbox payment steps.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual Palette**: Terracotta accent (`#be673b`/`#d97848`), soft cream background (`#f4f1ea`), warm dark charcoal text (`#171715`). Dark mode tints toward warm coffee colors.
- **Typography**: Display titles set in `Space Grotesk` for geometric structure; body content set in `Source Sans 3` for robust readability.
- **Composition**: Fluid layouts using asymmetric sidebars, custom-spaced flex layout grids rather than generic card wraps, and clear visual hierarchy.

### Design Principles
1. **Asymmetric Rhythm**: Avoid generic card-grids. Mix details, lists, and tables with varied spacing (tight card elements alongside open tables).
2. **Interactive Tactility**: Hover states should subtly lift elements, change background tints, or scale indicators. Active states should sink/press.
3. **No Decorative AI Slop**: No generic blue-to-purple gradients, no unnecessary glass card blurs, and no round-bordered left accents.
4. **Progressive Disclosure**: Detailed order logs and timeline items remain collapsed or tucked away until selected, keeping main views clean.
